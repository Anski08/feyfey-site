The problem

Video is the most passive way to study.

You watch a lecture, a procedure, a tutorial. It makes sense while it plays. A week later almost none of it is retrievable, because recognising something is not the same as recalling it.

Flashcard apps solved this for text. Video never got the same treatment — so the format people increasingly learn from is the one with the weakest retention tooling.

Recognition vs. recall

How it works

Three things, in order.

Add a video and write the question first

Pick a file from your device and give it a subject and a priority. Optionally add a before you watch question. FeyFey shows that question above the player, so you attempt the answer before the video gives it to you. That one reversal is what makes the session active rather than passive.

Bookmark the moments that matter

While it plays, label a moment — "side effects", "step 4", "the bit I always forget". FeyFey stores the label with its timestamp, and it becomes a chip you can tap to jump straight back to that second. Your notes point into the video instead of sitting beside it.

Rate it, and let the schedule decide

After reviewing, you answer one question: was that Forgot, Hard, Good, or Easy? Each option shows the interval it will produce before you choose it, so the schedule is never a black box. Material you know drifts weeks into the future; material you don't comes back tomorrow.

A typical interval sequence

Rating something Forgot resets it to one day and makes every future interval for that video slightly shorter. The exact arithmetic is on the how it's built page.

What you get

Built around one session a day.

A queue, not a library

Overdue first, then due today, then new. Capped at a daily goal you set, so the session always ends.

Bookmarks with timestamps

Tap a label to jump to that second. Your notes live inside the video, not in a separate document.

Teach it back

Record yourself explaining a topic, add it like any other video, and let it come back on the schedule. Nothing special is required — a teach-back is just a video you made.

Where your videos live

Your video files never reach our servers.

When you add a video, the file is stored by your browser on your own device. It is never uploaded. We hold no copy, we cannot watch it, and we could not hand it to anyone if we were asked.

What does sync is the small stuff: titles, subjects, bookmark labels and timestamps, and your review schedule. That is what lets you sign in elsewhere and keep your progress.

Two limits worth knowing before you start

Clearing your browser data deletes your videos. Because the files only exist on your device, there is no backup to restore from. Keep your originals somewhere safe.

Video files do not follow you between devices. Sign in on your phone and your library and schedule are there, but each file has to be added on each device you want to watch it on.

Why we built it this way

Hosting other people's study material means holding it, scanning it, and eventually handing it over. Keeping files local removes that entire category of risk — and it is also why FeyFey costs what it does.

Honest status

What works today, and what doesn't yet.

Working

  • Accounts and sign-in

  • Subjects, priorities and pre-watch questions
